Introduction for teachers

quizbinf
Peer instruction in the lecture hall

Students answer a question on their phones, discuss it with their neighbours, and answer the same question again. You see whether the discussion moved the class.

The method

Why every question is asked twice

Peer instruction (Mazur). The first vote measures what students think on their own; the second measures what they think after arguing about it.

  • Answer alone. Every student commits to an answer before hearing anyone else.
  • Discuss. Two to three minutes with the neighbours, ideally with someone who answered differently.
  • Answer again. Same question, same choices.
  • Compare. Before and after are shown side by side. Then the correct answer is revealed and explained.
The method works best with questions that 30 to 70 percent answer correctly the first time. Below that there is too little to discuss; above it there is no disagreement.

Before the lecture

Writing the questions

  • One quiz per lecture, created on the dashboard at /teacher.
  • Question text is Markdown, with a live preview rendered exactly as students will see it.
  • Figures are uploaded to the app; {width=60%} sizes them for both phone and projector.
  • Two kinds: multiple choice with exactly one correct choice, or a word cloud.
  • Nothing is marked correct by default. The form will not save until you choose.
  • Questions can be edited and reordered at any time, also after they have been asked.

During the lecture

Four views of one session

Press Run session on a quiz. Each view has its own URL, so you can keep Control on the laptop and drag Join or Report to the projector window.

Join

Projected

QR code and join count. Once the first bout opens, the question takes over and the QR shrinks to a corner for latecomers.

Control

Your screen

Opens and halts bouts. While a bout is open it shows only the live question and an answer count.

Report

Projected after halting

Before and after bars, word clouds, the name draw, and hand-outs for Canvas.

Participants

Never project

Per-student record. Names hidden until you click. Canvas attendance file.

Your laptop screen is often visible to the room while the projector is being set up. The app is written so that nothing you see by accident gives the answer away.

Step 1 · Students arrive

Scan, log in with KTH-id, wait

Join view, projected. The code also works typed by hand.

The student's phone follows the session live; no reload needed.

An opening question

Word cloud: “What do you expect to learn?”

  • One word or short phrase, at most 40 characters. Asked once, no correct answer.
  • The cloud appears only after you halt, so you see it before the room does.
  • “DNA” and “dna” are counted together.

Step 2 · First bout

Students answer alone; you see a count

Control view: “15 answers in”, no breakdown. Other questions are one click away.

A student can change the answer as long as the bout is open; the last one counts.

Step 2 · What the room sees

The question, not the votes

  • The Join view now shows the question with its choices, unmarked.
  • The QR code sits in the corner for those who failed to log in.
  • No distribution is shown while a bout is open. A visible split before discussion tells students what to answer.

Step 3 · Halt, then discuss

The first distribution, without the answer

  • After Halt submission the Report view shows the first bars.
  • No choice is marked correct at this point. Here, half the class believes the E-value is a probability of homology, which is a good starting point for discussion.
  • Ask students to find a neighbour who answered differently.

Step 4 · Optional

Draw two students to explain

  • Draw two to explain rolls names and stops on two students.
  • Only students who answered this question can be drawn, so nobody is asked to defend a position they never took.
  • Only names are shown, never what they chose. The student decides how much to reveal.
  • Somebody absent? Click Draw again.

Steps 5 to 7 · Second bout and reveal

Before and after, with the correct choice marked

  • Open the second bout from Control, wait, halt.
  • The Report view now shows both distributions and marks the correct choice with a tick.
  • In this example, correct answers went from 6 of 22 to 18 of 24.
  • Close by explaining why the wrong choices are wrong; that is what students remember.

After the lecture

Participation and Canvas

Participants view with names revealed. Teacher only; do not project.

  • Mark this lecture in Canvas downloads a gradebook file: one point if the student answered at least 75% of the bouts.
  • The point is for answering, not for being right. Correctness is never graded.
  • An end-of-term report on the dashboard covers all your sessions.
  • Hand out in the Report view exports the questions as HTML or Markdown for a Canvas page, with or without answers.

Rules the app enforces

What you can rely on

Answers only while a bout is open

Every submission outside the window is refused by the server, not just hidden by the phone.

No bars while a bout is open

Control shows a count. Distributions appear only after halting.

No tick until the second bout is halted

Neither the projected views nor the dashboard mark the correct choice before then, unless you click “Show which is correct”.

Students see only their own answer

The correct answer is not even sent to the phones, so it cannot be read from the browser.

Asked questions cannot be deleted

Answers are the only data that cannot be recreated. Editing wording or the correct choice is always allowed.

Reset discards answers

Control's Reset lets a question be run again, at the cost of its answers. Use it deliberately.

Practical advice

Running your first lecture

  • Rehearse with your own phone in a private window: open the session, answer, halt.
  • Two windows: Control on the laptop, Join or Report on the projector.
  • Keep bouts short. One to two minutes; halt when the count stops rising.
  • Leave the QR up for the first minutes. Somebody always fails to log in.
  • Three or four questions per lecture is enough; each takes about ten minutes with discussion.
  • Download the Canvas file after the lecture rather than at the end of term.
Known limitation. The six-character code can be sent by text message, so a student outside the room can answer during an open bout. Short windows limit this but do not prevent it.

Summary

Ask, discuss, ask again

Write questions on the dashboard. In class: project Join, drive from Control, show Report after each halt. Afterwards: one file to Canvas.
